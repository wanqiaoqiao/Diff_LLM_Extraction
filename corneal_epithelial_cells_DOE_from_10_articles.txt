==================================================
1. Consensus Intermediate Stage Map
==================================================

Stage A. Starting pluripotent state / pre-induction stabilization
Typical time range:
- Day -1 to Day 0

Cell state:
- iPSC / hiPSC / hPSC

Typical basal medium:
- mTeSR1, E8, Essential 8 Flex, RegES, or pluripotent maintenance medium

Common factors:
- Y-27632: 10 uM for plating survival
- or Blebbistatin: 5 uM in some xeno-free rapid protocols

Interpretation:
- This stage standardizes survival, attachment, and colony quality.
- It is rarely the main lineage-defining stage, but it strongly affects reproducibility of downstream corneal induction.

Stage B. Surface ectoderm / ocular surface induction
Typical time range:
- Day 0 to Day 4

Cell state:
- early surface ectoderm-like cells
- ocular surface ectoderm-like cells
- pre-corneal epithelial progenitor state

Typical basal medium families:
- suspension induction in xeno-free basal induction medium or modified RegES
- defined feeder-free induction medium

Common factors:
- SB-505124: typically 10 uM
- IWP-2: typically 10 uM in the small-molecule route
- bFGF: commonly 50 ng/mL
- BMP4: most often 25 ng/mL during Day 3-4 in rapid defined protocols
- IWR-1 or A83-01 in some defined variants

Typical markers:
- OCT4 down
- PAX6 begins to increase
- early epithelial commitment becomes visible after replating

Interpretation:
- This is the most conserved early lineage-specifying stage in the defined protocols.
- The common logic is to suppress non-corneal fates and bias toward surface ectoderm / ocular surface fate using TGF-beta and Wnt modulation with controlled FGF/BMP exposure.

Stage C. Early corneal epithelial progenitor / limbal-like progenitor induction
Typical time range:
- Day 4 to Day 10-12

Cell state:
- corneal epithelial progenitor-like cells
- limbal epithelial progenitor-like cells
- basal epithelial progenitor-like cells

Typical basal medium:
- CnT-30
- DKSFM
- DMEM/F12:KSFM mixtures
- CnT-Prime in some later maturation protocols

Typical matrices:
- Collagen IV: most recurrent, often 5 ug/cm2
- LN-521 + Collagen IV in xeno-free optimized protocols
- denuded amniotic membrane or denuded cornea in niche-oriented protocols

Typical markers:
- PAX6+
- p63 / DeltaNp63alpha+
- CK14+
- CK15+
- ABCG2 may be high in earlier progenitor windows
- OCT4 should continue to fall

Interpretation:
- This is the key controllable intermediate state for DOE.
- Papers differ in whether they call this limbal, corneal epithelial progenitor, or basal epithelial progenitor, but the marker logic is highly overlapping.

Stage D. Limbal stem-like enrichment / corneal epithelial commitment
Typical time range:
- Day 10-12 to Day 20-25

Cell state:
- limbal stem-like / limbal epithelial stem cell-like cells
- corneal epithelial lineage committed progenitors
- ABCB5+ / DeltaNp63alpha+ limbal-like cells

Common signals / conditions:
- continued culture in CnT-30 or similar corneal epithelial medium
- stable matrix support rather than aggressive re-patterning
- optional cell enrichment/sorting using marker combinations in SEAM-like workflows

Typical markers:
- DeltaNp63alpha+
- ABCB5+
- CK14+
- CK15+
- PAX6+
- low or absent CK12 in the more stem-like fraction
- OCT4 negative

Interpretation:
- This stage is often the most practical product state if the goal is a transplantable or expandable ocular surface progenitor population.
- Several later papers imply this stage predicts better wound healing, engraftment, and expandability than pushing immediately to full CK12-high maturation.

Stage E. Mature corneal epithelial cells / stratified corneal epithelial sheet
Typical time range:
- Day 20-25 onward to Day 35-45+
- much longer in SEAM and older co-culture systems, sometimes 10-15 weeks

Cell state:
- mature corneal epithelial-like cells
- stratified corneal epithelial cells
- transplantable corneal epithelial sheet

Common maturation-promoting conditions:
- KGF: often 10 ng/mL
- FBS: often 5% in terminal maturation conditions
- CaCl2: around 1 mM in stratification-promoting conditions
- air-lift or sheet culture in some protocols
- human amniotic membrane or denuded corneal substrate in niche-biased maturation

Typical markers:
- KRT12 / CK12
- KRT3 / CK3
- PAX6 retained
- layered epithelial morphology

Interpretation:
- This stage confirms that the progenitor state was correctly specified.
- But for optimizing the protocol, it is usually better to optimize Stage C-D first and use Stage E as a downstream validation stage.

==================================================
2. Factor and Concentration Ranges by Stage
==================================================

Stage A. Pre-induction stabilization
- Y-27632: 10 uM
- Blebbistatin: 5 uM
Reasoning:
- These are recurrent survival aids in the early plating or suspension phase.
- They are better treated as setup stabilizers than as major DOE variables in the first round.

Stage B. Surface ectoderm / ocular surface induction
- SB-505124: around 10 uM
- IWP-2: around 10 uM
- bFGF: around 50 ng/mL
- BMP4: around 25 ng/mL, with a practical testable range of 10-50 ng/mL
- IWR-1: used in some defined protocols
- A83-01: used in some defined protocols
Reasoning:
- Across defined protocols, this is the strongest common induction module.
- The signaling logic is more stable than the exact medium brand name: control TGF-beta/Wnt, add carefully timed FGF/BMP cues, then transition to corneal epithelial medium.

Stage C-D. Early corneal epithelial progenitor / limbal-like commitment
- Collagen IV coating: commonly 5 ug/cm2
- LN-521: commonly combined at about 0.5 ug/cm2 in xeno-free optimized systems
- Corneal epithelial media: CnT-30, DKSFM, CnT-Prime, DMEM/F12:KSFM
Reasoning:
- The matrix and medium switch are central at this stage.
- Collagen IV is the most reproducible matrix signal across multiple articles.
- LN-521 appears to improve robustness and translational compatibility in feeder-free systems.

Stage E. Terminal maturation / stratification
- KGF: around 10 ng/mL
- FBS: around 5%
- CaCl2: around 1 mM
Reasoning:
- These factors recur mainly in later maturation or sheet formation.
- They should not dominate first-pass optimization if the main goal is first to build a reproducible corneal epithelial lineage trajectory.

Niche-driven family factors that are harder to standardize:
- Conditioned medium from limbal fibroblasts or limbal stromal cells
- PA6 co-culture
- denuded amniotic membrane
- denuded corneal scaffold
Reasoning:
- These methods clearly work biologically, but they introduce batch effects and poorly defined variables.
- They are useful benchmark arms, but not ideal as the center of a first DOE.

==================================================
3. Recommended Working Definition of the Intermediate and Final Endpoints
==================================================

For this project, I recommend using two operational endpoints.

Primary optimization endpoint:
- Day 20-25 limbal/corneal epithelial progenitor-like population

Primary positive markers:
- DeltaNp63alpha
- PAX6
- CK14
- CK15
- ABCB5 if measurable
- ABCG2 if focusing on the earlier progenitor window

Primary negative / exclusion markers:
- OCT4 residual pluripotent cells
- low non-epithelial or non-corneal colonies
- low neural contamination if checked
- low keratinization / epidermal drift if checked

Secondary maturation endpoint:
- Day 35-45 mature corneal epithelial-like cells / epithelial sheet

Secondary positive markers:
- KRT12 / CK12
- KRT3 / CK3
- PAX6
- multilayered epithelial morphology

Why this two-endpoint definition:
- The selected papers repeatedly show that the limbal-like progenitor window is more reproducible and more actionable than immediate full maturation.
- Mature CK12+/CK3+ corneal epithelial identity is still necessary, but it is more sensitive to late culture conditions and substrate effects.
- Therefore the best DOE structure is to optimize the Stage C-D population first, then validate whether it matures efficiently into Stage E.

==================================================
4. Core Logic for the DOE Structure
==================================================

The 10 papers do not describe one single protocol family.
They cluster into three main strategy families.

Family A:
- niche-driven co-culture / conditioned-medium methods
- PA6 feeder logic, limbal fibroblast conditioned medium, limbal stromal conditioned medium, HAM or denuded corneal substrate

Family B:
- defined rapid induction methods
- brief early suspension or induction phase with small molecules and growth factors, followed by adherent corneal epithelial differentiation on Collagen IV or LN-521/Collagen IV

Family C:
- SEAM / self-organizing ocular surface routes
- longer duration, self-patterning ocular ectoderm architecture, later zone-based isolation and sorting

Why this matters:
- If you mix all three families into one first-pass DOE, interpretation becomes muddy.
- The variables are not simple dose tweaks of the same developmental logic.
- The most practical and scalable first DOE should be built around Family B, then benchmarked against one niche-based reference condition and optionally one SEAM-derived reference condition.

My recommendation:
- Use the defined rapid induction family as the DOE backbone.
- Use a niche-based arm only as a biological comparator.
- Reserve SEAM-style optimization for a second campaign if you want the highest-quality sheet product or highly purified corneal epithelial lineage cells.

5. Proposed DOE Framework
==================================================

Stage 0. Route-selection mini DOE
Objective:
- Decide whether your iPSC line behaves best in a defined rapid protocol, a niche-assisted protocol, or a self-organizing long protocol.

Route A baseline:
- Defined rapid induction
- Blebbistatin 5 uM or Y-27632 10 uM
- Day 1: SB-505124 10 uM + bFGF 50 ng/mL
- Day 3-4: BMP4 25 ng/mL
- Day 4 onward: adherent culture on Collagen IV or LN-521 + Collagen IV in CnT-30

Route B baseline:
- Defined small-molecule route
- SB-505124 10 uM + IWP-2 10 uM + bFGF 50 ng/mL for early induction
- then long adherent differentiation in CnT-30

Route C reference:
- niche-driven comparator such as limbal conditioned medium / denuded cornea or HAM-supported maturation

Primary readout at Day 10-12:
- PAX6+
- DeltaNp63alpha+
- CK14+
- ABCG2+
- OCT4 residual low
- morphology consistency / attachment

Decision rule:
- Pick the route that gives the cleanest and most reproducible corneal epithelial progenitor identity, not merely the highest late CK12 alone.

==================================================
6. DOE A: Optimization within the Defined Rapid Induction Family
==================================================

Objective:
- Maximize clean corneal epithelial progenitor / limbal-like progenitor generation by Day 20-25.

Primary response variables:
- DeltaNp63alpha+ percentage
- PAX6+ percentage
- CK14+ percentage
- CK15+ percentage
- ABCB5+ percentage if available
- OCT4 residual percentage
- viable yield / adherence consistency

Secondary response variables:
- ABCG2 at Day 10-12
- CK12 and CK3 at Day 35-45 as downstream validation only

Screening factors:

Factor A. Early survival module
Levels:
- Y-27632 10 uM
- Blebbistatin 5 uM
Logic:
- Both are recurrent early stabilizers.
- This is a simple but useful branch variable because some lines tolerate suspension and aggregate handling better with one than the other.

Factor B. TGF-beta / Wnt induction implementation
Levels:
- SB-505124 10 uM + bFGF 50 ng/mL
- SB-505124 10 uM + IWP-2 10 uM + bFGF 50 ng/mL
- IWR-1 + A83-01 + bFGF style defined induction arm
Logic:
- These represent the main defined family variants reported across the selected papers.
- This factor tests whether stronger Wnt suppression improves your line or overconstrains it.

Factor C. BMP4 concentration
Levels:
- 10 ng/mL
- 25 ng/mL
- 50 ng/mL
Logic:
- 25 ng/mL is the clearest literature center.
- A broader 10-50 ng/mL range captures whether your line prefers milder or stronger surface ectoderm push.

Factor D. Matrix
Levels:
- Collagen IV only
- LN-521 + Collagen IV
Logic:
- Matrix is one of the most reproducible determinants of attachment and progenitor identity.
- LN-521 + Collagen IV appears attractive for robustness and translational compatibility.

Factor E. Adherent differentiation medium
Levels:
- CnT-30
- DKSFM
- DMEM/F12:KSFM (1:1)
Logic:
- The field repeatedly reuses these few medium families.
- This is a real practical variable and not just a vendor preference.

Factor F. Progenitor harvest day
Levels:
- Day 12
- Day 20
- Day 25
Logic:
- Early readout captures ABCG2-rich progenitor bias.
- Later readout captures DeltaNp63alpha/ABCB5-enriched limbal-like populations.
- This helps define whether your product should be harvested earlier or matured longer.

Hold fixed during DOE A:
- plating density and confluence strategy
- one matrix-precoat method within each matrix arm
- no FBS or CaCl2 in the early optimization phase
- no niche-conditioned medium in the first-pass DOE

==================================================
7. DOE B: Terminal Maturation / Sheet Formation Optimization
==================================================

Objective:
- Starting from the best Stage C-D condition, maximize mature corneal epithelial identity and sheet quality.

Primary response variables:
- CK12+ percentage
- CK3+ percentage
- layered epithelial morphology
- sheet integrity / handling quality
- continued PAX6 expression

Screening factors:

Factor A. KGF
Levels:
- 0 ng/mL
- 10 ng/mL
Logic:
- KGF is a recurring maturation-support cue in late epithelial differentiation.

Factor B. Calcium / serum maturation module
Levels:
- none
- 1 mM CaCl2
- 5% FBS + 1 mM CaCl2
Logic:
- This factor tests whether stronger stratification cues improve maturation without causing non-corneal drift.

Factor C. Substrate for maturation
Levels:
- standard coated plastic
- amniotic membrane / denuded corneal substrate benchmark arm
Logic:
- Substrate dependence is especially important at the final maturation stage.
- This is where niche-based methods can outperform defined plastic-only culture for stratification.

Factor D. Maturation duration
Levels:
- 14 days
- 21 days
- 28 days
Logic:
- Some lines acquire CK12/CK3 relatively early, whereas others need longer stratification time.

Hold fixed during DOE B:
- use the best DOE A progenitor-induction condition
- do not re-open early signaling variables

8. Minimal Practical DOE if You Want One First Campaign Only
==================================================

If you want one compact campaign instead of two sequential campaigns, I recommend a reduced DOE around the defined rapid induction family.

Recommended reduced factor set:
- BMP4: 10 / 25 / 50 ng/mL
- Matrix: Collagen IV vs LN-521 + Collagen IV
- Adherent medium: CnT-30 vs DMEM/F12:KSFM
- Harvest day: Day 12 vs Day 25

Keep fixed:
- Blebbistatin 5 uM in the early phase
- SB-505124 10 uM + bFGF 50 ng/mL
- optional add-on arm with IWP-2 10 uM only if you have room

Primary readout:
- Day 12: PAX6, DeltaNp63alpha, CK14, ABCG2, OCT4
- Day 25: DeltaNp63alpha, ABCB5, CK14, CK15, OCT4
- Optional Day 45: CK12, CK3

Why this compact DOE:
- It keeps the highest-signal controllable variables.
- It avoids mixing poorly defined niche variables into the first pass.
- It gives you both an early and a later progenitor window before committing to full terminal maturation.

==================================================
9. Practical Anchor Protocol to Start From
==================================================

Recommended anchor protocol:
- Day -1 to 0: seed healthy iPSC/hPSC at controlled confluence with Y-27632 10 uM or Blebbistatin 5 uM
- Day 1: start defined early induction with SB-505124 10 uM + bFGF 50 ng/mL
- Day 3-4: add BMP4 25 ng/mL
- Day 4 onward: plate onto Collagen IV 5 ug/cm2 or LN-521 + Collagen IV
- Day 4-24: maintain in CnT-30 or comparable corneal epithelial medium
- Day 10-12: assess PAX6, DeltaNp63alpha, CK14, ABCG2, OCT4
- Day 20-25: assess DeltaNp63alpha, ABCB5, CK14, CK15, OCT4
- Day 25-45 if desired: maturation with KGF 10 ng/mL and test +/- 1 mM CaCl2, +/- 5% FBS for CK12/CK3 induction

Why this anchor protocol:
- It sits near the center of the most reproducible defined-protocol literature.
- It preserves a limbal-like progenitor window before forcing late maturation.
- It is easier to scale and interpret than conditioned-medium or long SEAM-only workflows.

==================================================
10. Logic, Evidence, and Self-Check
==================================================

Main synthesis logic:
- The strongest consensus across the articles is not one exact vendor medium but one developmental sequence:
  1. stabilize pluripotent cells,
  2. bias toward surface ectoderm / ocular surface fate,
  3. commit into a limbal/corneal epithelial progenitor state,
  4. only then drive terminal corneal epithelial maturation.
- The most reproducible common variables are early signaling control, matrix choice, and the switch into corneal epithelial medium.
- The most variable variables are late maturation substrate and late stratification cues.

Why I prioritize Stage C-D in the DOE:
- Day 20-25 limbal-like progenitors are easier to compare across conditions than Day 45 mature sheets.
- These intermediate cells are the point where most protocol families still overlap biologically.
- If this stage is weak, late CK12/CK3 positivity becomes noisy and hard to interpret.

Why I do not center the first DOE on niche-conditioned medium or PA6:
- Those systems are biologically meaningful but poorly defined.
- Batch effects make concentration-range DOE interpretation difficult.
- They are better used as benchmark comparators than as the DOE backbone.

Why I separate terminal maturation into DOE B:
- Late maturation depends strongly on substrate and sheet handling.
- If you optimize everything at once, you will not know whether failures come from patterning or from stratification.

Main evidence threads behind this summary:
- Small-molecule and defined feeder-free papers converge on TGF-beta/Wnt modulation, Collagen IV-based adhesion, and corneal epithelial media.
- Xeno-free rapid protocols converge on a brief early induction window followed by a longer adherent corneal epithelial differentiation phase.
- SEAM and cell-sheet papers show that the later harvest/sorting strategy matters, but they also reinforce that the useful corneal lineage state appears well before final mature sheet transplantation.
- Translational papers reinforce the value of preserving limbal stem-like traits such as DeltaNp63alpha and ABCB5 before or alongside terminal CK12 induction.

Self-check and limitations:
- This field has fewer clean, high-throughput protocol papers than cardiomyocyte or dopaminergic differentiation.
- Some papers emphasize limbal stem-like endpoints more than mature central corneal epithelial endpoints.
- Marker naming varies across papers: CK3/KRT3, CK12/KRT12, p63/DeltaNp63alpha, limbal-like versus corneal epithelial progenitor.
- I therefore treated the lineage as a staged continuum rather than forcing one rigid nomenclature.
- The most likely first source of lab-to-lab variability will be cell-line responsiveness and matrix/substrate effects.

Bottom-line recommendation:
- Start with a defined rapid induction protocol.
- Optimize the limbal/corneal epithelial progenitor window first.
- Then run a second-stage maturation DOE to maximize CK12/CK3 and sheet quality.
