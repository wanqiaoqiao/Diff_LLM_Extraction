==================================================
1. Consensus Intermediate Stage Map
==================================================

Stage A. Starting pluripotent state / pre-induction stabilization
Typical time range:
- Day -1 to Day 0

Cell state:
- iPSC / hiPSC / hPSC

Typical basal medium:
- mTeSR1, E8, StemFlex, or equivalent pluripotent maintenance medium

Common factors:
- Y-27632: 10 uM for single-cell plating or stressful replating when used

Interpretation:
- This stage standardizes survival, confluence, and colony quality.
- It is not the main lineage-defining stage, but it strongly affects definitive endoderm efficiency.

Stage B. Definitive endoderm induction
Typical time range:
- Day 0 to Day 3

Cell state:
- definitive endoderm

Typical basal medium families:
- RPMI-based medium
- MCDB131-based medium
- serum-free chemically defined medium variants

Common factors:
- Activin A: commonly 100 ng/mL
- Wnt activation: CHIR99021 about 3 uM for 1 day, or Wnt3a in older systems
- low serum or B27 minus insulin in some systems

Typical markers:
- SOX17+
- FOXA2+
- CXCR4+
- c-KIT+
- OCT4 down

Interpretation:
- This is the first major gate for success.
- Poor definitive endoderm efficiency almost always limits all downstream pancreatic and beta-cell outcomes.

Stage C. Primitive gut tube / gut endoderm transition
Typical time range:
- Day 3 to Day 5-6

Cell state:
- primitive gut tube / gut tube endoderm

Typical factors:
- FGF7 / KGF: commonly 50 ng/mL
- sometimes continued basal endoderm medium without strong Activin A

Typical markers:
- HNF1B
- HNF4A
- FOXA2 retained
- SOX17 begins to decline depending on timing

Interpretation:
- This is a short transition stage.
- It prepares definitive endoderm for posterior foregut and pancreatic specification.

Stage D. Posterior foregut / pancreatic foregut specification
Typical time range:
- Day 5-6 to Day 8-9

Cell state:
- posterior foregut
- pancreatic foregut
- early pancreatic progenitor-biased endoderm

Common factors:
- Retinoic acid: commonly 2 uM
- SANT-1 or other Hedgehog inhibitor: commonly around 0.25 uM
- LDN193189 or other BMP inhibition: commonly around 100-200 nM
- PKC activation such as TPB: commonly around 200 nM in several six-stage protocols
- FGF7/KGF may continue in some protocols

Typical markers:
- PDX1 begins to increase
- HNF6 / ONECUT1
- HNF1B
- SOX9 begins to emerge

Interpretation:
- This is one of the highest-sensitivity patterning stages.
- The balance of RA, Hedgehog inhibition, BMP inhibition, and PKC activation strongly affects PDX1 induction and later NKX6.1 competence.

Stage E. Pancreatic progenitor generation
Typical time range:
- Day 8-9 to Day 12-14

Cell state:
- PDX1+ pancreatic progenitor
- PDX1+NKX6.1+ pancreatic progenitor
- multipotent pancreatic progenitor-like cell

Common factors:
- EGF: often 50 ng/mL
- KGF / FGF7: often 50 ng/mL
- Nicotinamide: often 10 mM in some protocols
- ALK5 inhibitor / TGF-beta pathway inhibition: Alk5i II commonly 10 uM or related inhibitors
- continued RA or SHH/BMP inhibition may be protocol-specific and usually tapers off

Typical markers:
- PDX1+
- NKX6.1+
- SOX9+
- HNF6+
- PTF1A in some pancreatic progenitor contexts

Interpretation:
- This is the key intermediate checkpoint for DOE.
- High PDX1 alone is not enough; PDX1+NKX6.1+ is usually the more predictive progenitor state for beta-cell competence.

Stage F. Endocrine progenitor induction
Typical time range:
- Day 12-14 to Day 18-21

Cell state:
- endocrine progenitor
- NEUROG3+ endocrine precursor
- early insulin-lineage committed cells

Common factors:
- Notch inhibition via gamma-secretase inhibitor such as DAPT: often about 1-10 uM depending on protocol
- ALK5 inhibitor: often around 10 uM
- thyroid hormone T3: commonly around 1 uM in maturation-oriented protocols
- XXI / gamma-secretase inhibitor or related endocrine induction compounds in some protocols
- low or removed KGF/EGF depending on the protocol

Typical markers:
- NEUROG3+
- NKX2.2+
- NEUROD1+
- CHGA+
- ISL1+
- early INS begins to appear

Interpretation:
- This stage decides whether pancreatic progenitors become endocrine cells.
- Over- or under-inhibition of Notch can change endocrine yield and may also affect proliferation and maturity.

Stage G. Immature beta-like cell formation
Typical time range:
- Day 18-21 to Day 28-35

Cell state:
- immature beta-like cells
- insulin-positive endocrine cells
- polyhormonal or monohormonal endocrine cells depending on protocol quality

Common factors:
- T3: around 1 uM
- ALK5 inhibitor: around 10 uM
- gamma-secretase inhibitor may be removed or continued briefly depending on system
- heparin: often 10 ug/mL in some maturation media
- betacellulin / EGF family factors in some older systems
- zinc, ascorbic acid, or other maturation-support supplements in some protocols

Typical markers:
- INS+
- C-peptide+
- NKX6.1+
- PDX1+
- NKX2.2+
- MAFA low or emerging
- glucagon should decrease if beta identity improves

Interpretation:
- This stage is where many protocols appear successful by insulin staining but still fail mature glucose-stimulated insulin secretion.
- A DOE should not use INS positivity alone as the final success metric.

Stage H. Mature functional beta-like cell / stem-cell-derived islet maturation
Typical time range:
- Day 28-35 to Day 45-60+

Cell state:
- beta-like cell
- stem-cell-derived beta cell
- stem-cell-derived islet / SC-islet cluster

Common maturation conditions:
- 3D cluster culture
- air-liquid interface or suspension cluster maturation in some systems
- T3 support
- ALK5 inhibition in defined windows
- extended culture with low mitogen pressure
- ECM or aggregation-state optimization

Typical markers:
- INS+
- C-peptide+
- PDX1+
- NKX6.1+
- MAFA+
- UCN3 emerging in better maturation states
- low GCG/SST contamination ideally

Functional readouts:
- glucose-stimulated insulin secretion, GSIS
- stimulation index
- dynamic perifusion if available
- insulin content
- proinsulin/insulin ratio
- transplantation reversal of hyperglycemia in animal models when relevant

Interpretation:
- This stage validates that the earlier progenitor and endocrine stages were correct.
- But for DOE purposes, Stages B-F are the most diagnostic process-control stages, and Stage H is the final functional validation.

2. Factor and Concentration Ranges by Stage
==================================================

Stage A. Pre-induction stabilization
- Y-27632: 10 uM
Reasoning:
- Useful for survival during single-cell plating or stressful passaging.
- Should usually be fixed rather than optimized in the first beta-cell DOE.

Stage B. Definitive endoderm
- Activin A: 100 ng/mL
- CHIR99021: about 2-4 uM, practical center 3 uM, often for the first 24 h
- Wnt3a: older alternative to CHIR-based Wnt activation
Reasoning:
- High Activin A plus transient Wnt activation is the strongest consensus early step.
- The main practical tuning variable is Wnt dose and exposure duration, because excessive Wnt can reduce downstream pancreatic competence.

Stage C. Primitive gut tube
- KGF / FGF7: about 50 ng/mL
Reasoning:
- KGF/FGF7 is highly recurrent for gut tube and early pancreatic lineage progression.
- This is usually a lower-risk fixed factor in a first DOE.

Stage D. Posterior foregut / pancreatic foregut
- Retinoic acid: about 1-2 uM, practical center 2 uM
- SANT-1: about 0.25 uM
- LDN193189: about 100-200 nM
- TPB / PKC activator: about 200 nM
Reasoning:
- This is one of the highest-impact windows for PDX1 induction and later NKX6.1 competence.
- RA dose/timing and BMP/Hedgehog inhibition are especially important.

Stage E. Pancreatic progenitor
- EGF: about 50 ng/mL
- KGF / FGF7: about 50 ng/mL
- Nicotinamide: about 10 mM in protocols that use it
- ALK5 inhibitor: about 10 uM when used
Reasoning:
- The main goal is to maximize PDX1+NKX6.1+ cells, not simply PDX1+ cells.
- This stage is a strong DOE target because progenitor quality predicts final beta-cell competence.

Stage F. Endocrine progenitor
- DAPT or gamma-secretase inhibitor: about 1-10 uM
- ALK5 inhibitor: about 10 uM
- T3: about 1 uM
Reasoning:
- Notch inhibition controls endocrine induction.
- TGF-beta inhibition and T3 help transition toward endocrine/beta maturation, but timing matters.

Stage G-H. Beta-like maturation
- T3: about 1 uM
- ALK5 inhibitor: about 10 uM in defined windows
- heparin: about 10 ug/mL in some protocols
- 3D cluster culture: categorical variable rather than concentration
Reasoning:
- Late maturation depends strongly on aggregate state and timing.
- Maturation factors should be optimized after progenitor and endocrine induction are reliable.

3. Recommended Working Definition of the Intermediate and Final Endpoints
==================================================

For this project, I recommend using four operational endpoints.

Primary optimization endpoint 1:
- Day 3 definitive endoderm

Primary positive markers:
- SOX17
- FOXA2
- CXCR4
- c-KIT

Primary negative / exclusion markers:
- residual OCT4
- poor viability

Primary optimization endpoint 2:
- Day 12-14 pancreatic progenitor

Primary positive markers:
- PDX1
- NKX6.1
- SOX9
- HNF6 / ONECUT1

Primary negative / exclusion markers:
- PDX1+NKX6.1-negative excess if beta lineage is the goal
- off-target hepatic or intestinal markers if measured

Primary optimization endpoint 3:
- Day 18-21 endocrine progenitor

Primary positive markers:
- NEUROG3
- NKX2.2
- NEUROD1
- CHGA
- ISL1

Primary optimization endpoint 4:
- Day 35-60 beta-like functional endpoint

Primary positive markers:
- INS
- C-peptide
- PDX1
- NKX6.1
- NKX2.2
- MAFA
- UCN3 if maturation is strong

Functional readouts:
- GSIS stimulation index
- insulin content
- proinsulin/insulin ratio
- dynamic insulin secretion if available

Why this endpoint structure:
- Final insulin staining alone is insufficient.
- A strong DOE should identify whether failure occurs at endoderm induction, pancreatic progenitor formation, endocrine induction, or final maturation.
- PDX1+NKX6.1+ progenitors and NEUROG3+ endocrine progenitors are especially useful decision points.

4. Core Logic for the DOE Structure
==================================================

The selected papers cluster into three main strategy families.

Family A:
- classic six-stage directed differentiation
- definitive endoderm -> primitive gut tube -> posterior foregut -> pancreatic progenitor -> endocrine progenitor -> beta-like maturation

Family B:
- pancreatic progenitor optimization family
- focuses on maximizing PDX1+NKX6.1+ before endocrine induction

Family C:
- maturation / SC-islet / 3D cluster family
- focuses on improving glucose responsiveness, MAFA/UCN3 expression, and transplantation performance

Why this matters:
- If you optimize all stages at once, interpretation becomes muddy.
- Early defects in endoderm or pancreatic progenitor formation can be hidden by late insulin staining.
- Therefore DOE should be staged: first optimize developmental checkpoints, then optimize maturation.

My recommendation:
- Use a six-stage directed differentiation backbone.
- First optimize definitive endoderm and PDX1+NKX6.1+ pancreatic progenitor generation.
- Then optimize endocrine induction.
- Only after that, optimize maturation and 3D cluster conditions.

5. Proposed DOE Framework
==================================================

Stage 0. Backbone selection mini DOE
Objective:
- Decide whether your iPSC line behaves best in a standard six-stage monolayer-to-cluster route, a pancreatic progenitor-enriched route, or an early 3D aggregate route.

Route A baseline:
- Day 0-3: Activin A 100 ng/mL + CHIR99021 3 uM for early Wnt activation
- Day 3-5: KGF/FGF7 50 ng/mL
- Day 5-8: RA 2 uM + SANT-1 0.25 uM + LDN193189 100-200 nM + TPB 200 nM
- Day 8-14: EGF/KGF and ALK5/Nicotinamide-style pancreatic progenitor support
- Day 14-21: DAPT/gamma-secretase inhibition + ALK5 inhibition + T3
- Day 21 onward: 3D beta-like maturation

Route B baseline:
- same early endoderm and gut tube stages
- extended optimization of Day 8-14 pancreatic progenitor stage before endocrine induction

Route C baseline:
- same early specification but earlier aggregation / cluster maturation after endocrine induction

Primary readout:
- Day 3 SOX17/FOXA2/CXCR4
- Day 12-14 PDX1/NKX6.1
- Day 18-21 NEUROG3/NKX2.2
- Day 35-60 C-peptide/MAFA/GSIS

Decision rule:
- Pick the route that gives the strongest PDX1+NKX6.1+ progenitor state and endocrine induction, not simply the highest late insulin staining.

6. DOE A: Definitive Endoderm and Pancreatic Progenitor Optimization
==================================================

Objective:
- Maximize high-quality definitive endoderm and PDX1+NKX6.1+ pancreatic progenitors.

Primary response variables:
- Day 3 SOX17+FOXA2+ percentage
- Day 3 CXCR4+c-KIT+ percentage
- Day 12-14 PDX1+NKX6.1+ percentage
- viability / yield

Screening factors:

Factor A. CHIR99021 concentration during definitive endoderm induction
Levels:
- 2 uM
- 3 uM
- 4 uM
Logic:
- Wnt activation is necessary but dose-sensitive.
- 3 uM is a practical center, but line-to-line differences are common.

Factor B. CHIR99021 exposure duration
Levels:
- 18 h
- 24 h
- 36 h
Logic:
- Exposure duration can be as important as dose.
- Excessive Wnt can damage later pancreatic competence even if endoderm markers look good.

Factor C. Retinoic acid concentration in posterior foregut stage
Levels:
- 1 uM
- 2 uM
- 3 uM
Logic:
- RA is central for pancreatic foregut identity.
- Too little may reduce PDX1; too much may alter downstream patterning.

Factor D. BMP inhibition strength
Levels:
- LDN193189 100 nM
- LDN193189 200 nM
- alternative BMP inhibitor arm if available
Logic:
- BMP inhibition helps pancreatic specification, but over-inhibition can affect growth and survival.

Factor E. PKC activation strength
Levels:
- TPB 100 nM
- TPB 200 nM
- TPB 300 nM
Logic:
- PKC activation is a recurrent component of efficient pancreatic progenitor protocols.
- It likely interacts with RA and Hedgehog/BMP inhibition.

Factor F. Pancreatic progenitor harvest day
Levels:
- Day 11
- Day 13
- Day 15
Logic:
- PDX1 and NKX6.1 kinetics vary by cell line.
- A fixed day may miss the optimal progenitor window.

Hold fixed during DOE A:
- Activin A at 100 ng/mL
- KGF/FGF7 at 50 ng/mL
- SANT-1 around 0.25 uM as a practical center
- do not optimize late maturation factors yet

==================================================
7. DOE B: Endocrine Induction Optimization
==================================================

Objective:
- Starting from the best DOE A pancreatic progenitor condition, maximize endocrine progenitor and early beta-lineage commitment.

Primary response variables:
- NEUROG3+ percentage
- NKX2.2+ percentage
- NEUROD1+ percentage
- CHGA+ endocrine percentage
- early C-peptide+ percentage
- low polyhormonal GCG+/INS+ contamination if measured

Screening factors:

Factor A. Notch inhibition strength
Levels:
- DAPT 1 uM
- DAPT 5 uM
- DAPT 10 uM
Logic:
- Notch inhibition is central for endocrine induction.
- Too weak may reduce endocrine yield; too strong may reduce viability or distort timing.

Factor B. Notch inhibition timing
Levels:
- start Day 12
- start Day 14
- start Day 16
Logic:
- Endocrine induction should match pancreatic progenitor maturity.
- Starting too early can reduce progenitor expansion; starting too late can miss competence.

Factor C. ALK5 inhibitor timing
Levels:
- early endocrine window only
- endocrine plus early maturation
- late maturation only
Logic:
- TGF-beta inhibition supports endocrine/beta maturation but timing is important.

Factor D. T3 inclusion
Levels:
- 0 uM
- 1 uM
Logic:
- T3 is recurrent in maturation-oriented protocols.
- It is useful to test whether T3 should begin during endocrine induction or only later.

Hold fixed during DOE B:
- use the best DOE A progenitor condition
- keep aggregation state fixed until DOE C

8. DOE C: Beta-Like Maturation and Functional Optimization
==================================================

Objective:
- Starting from the best endocrine induction condition, maximize functional beta-like maturation.

Primary response variables:
- C-peptide+NKX6.1+ percentage
- MAFA+ percentage
- UCN3+ percentage if available
- GSIS stimulation index
- insulin content
- proinsulin/insulin ratio

Screening factors:

Factor A. Culture format
Levels:
- monolayer continuation
- 3D cluster after endocrine induction
- 3D cluster earlier at endocrine induction start
Logic:
- Aggregation state is one of the strongest late maturation variables.

Factor B. ALK5 inhibitor exposure window
Levels:
- none
- Day 18-28
- Day 18-35
Logic:
- TGF-beta inhibition is often useful but may become stage-dependent.

Factor C. T3 exposure window
Levels:
- none
- Day 18-35
- Day 18-50
Logic:
- T3 supports maturation, but excessive or mistimed exposure may not improve function.

Factor D. Heparin inclusion
Levels:
- 0 ug/mL
- 10 ug/mL
Logic:
- Heparin appears in some maturation media and may support cluster function.

Factor E. Maturation duration
Levels:
- Day 35 endpoint
- Day 45 endpoint
- Day 60 endpoint
Logic:
- Functional maturation is time-dependent.
- Some marker-positive cells require extended culture for GSIS improvement.

Hold fixed during DOE C:
- use the best DOE A and DOE B conditions
- do not reopen early endoderm/pancreatic patterning variables

9. Minimal Practical DOE if You Want One First Campaign Only
==================================================

If you want one compact campaign instead of three sequential campaigns, I recommend a reduced DOE focused on the highest-signal variables.

Recommended reduced factor set:
- CHIR99021: 2 / 3 / 4 uM during definitive endoderm
- RA: 1 / 2 / 3 uM during posterior foregut
- TPB: 100 / 200 / 300 nM during pancreatic foregut
- DAPT: 1 / 5 / 10 uM during endocrine induction
- culture format: monolayer vs 3D cluster after endocrine induction

Keep fixed:
- Activin A 100 ng/mL
- KGF/FGF7 50 ng/mL
- SANT-1 0.25 uM
- LDN193189 100-200 nM
- ALK5 inhibitor 10 uM in the chosen endocrine/maturation window
- T3 1 uM in the chosen maturation window

Primary readout:
- Day 3: SOX17, FOXA2, CXCR4, c-KIT
- Day 12-14: PDX1, NKX6.1, SOX9
- Day 18-21: NEUROG3, NKX2.2, NEUROD1, CHGA
- Day 35-60: C-peptide, INS, NKX6.1, MAFA, UCN3, GSIS

Why this compact DOE:
- It captures one critical variable in each major developmental window.
- It avoids making the first campaign too large to interpret.
- It can reveal whether the bottleneck is early endoderm, pancreatic progenitor formation, endocrine induction, or final maturation.

10. Practical Anchor Protocol to Start From
==================================================

Recommended anchor protocol:
- Day -1 to 0: seed healthy iPSC/hPSC at controlled density; use Y-27632 10 uM only if needed for survival
- Day 0-1: Activin A 100 ng/mL + CHIR99021 3 uM
- Day 1-3: Activin A 100 ng/mL without strong Wnt activation
- Day 3-5: KGF/FGF7 50 ng/mL to generate primitive gut tube
- Day 5-8: RA 2 uM + SANT-1 0.25 uM + LDN193189 100-200 nM + TPB 200 nM for posterior foregut/pancreatic foregut
- Day 8-14: pancreatic progenitor support with EGF/KGF-centered medium and ALK5/Nicotinamide-style support depending on platform
- Day 12-14: assess PDX1+NKX6.1+ pancreatic progenitors before endocrine induction
- Day 14-21: endocrine induction with DAPT/gamma-secretase inhibition + ALK5 inhibitor + T3
- Day 21 onward: transition to beta-like maturation, preferably testing 3D cluster maturation
- Day 35-60: assess C-peptide, INS, PDX1, NKX6.1, MAFA, UCN3, and GSIS

Why this anchor protocol:
- It sits near the center of the dominant six-stage hPSC-to-beta-cell literature.
- It makes each developmental checkpoint measurable.
- It avoids optimizing late GSIS before the progenitor and endocrine stages are known to be strong.

==================================================
11. Logic, Evidence, and Self-Check
==================================================

Main synthesis logic:
- The strongest consensus across the articles is a staged developmental sequence:
  1. stabilize pluripotent cells,
  2. induce definitive endoderm,
  3. transition through primitive gut tube,
  4. specify posterior foregut / pancreatic foregut,
  5. generate PDX1+NKX6.1+ pancreatic progenitors,
  6. induce NEUROG3+ endocrine progenitors,
  7. mature into beta-like cells or SC-islet clusters.
- The most reproducible early variables are Activin A, Wnt/CHIR timing, KGF/FGF7, RA, Hedgehog inhibition, BMP inhibition, and PKC activation.
- The most variable variables are endocrine induction timing, Notch inhibition strength, aggregation state, and late maturation duration.

Why I prioritize Stages B-F in the DOE:
- Day 3, Day 12-14, and Day 18-21 checkpoints are more diagnostic than final insulin staining alone.
- These intermediate states identify whether failure comes from endoderm, pancreatic progenitor formation, or endocrine induction.
- If these stages are weak, late GSIS optimization becomes noisy and misleading.

Why I separate maturation into DOE C:
- Late beta-cell function depends strongly on aggregation, maturation duration, and assay conditions.
- If you optimize maturation before fixing progenitor quality, you cannot know whether poor GSIS reflects poor beta-cell identity or poor maturation conditions.

Main evidence threads behind this summary:
- Multiple hPSC beta-cell papers converge on high-Activin definitive endoderm plus transient Wnt activation.
- Six-stage protocols converge on KGF/FGF7, RA, Hedgehog inhibition, BMP inhibition, and PKC activation for pancreatic foregut and pancreatic progenitor formation.
- Pancreatic progenitor studies emphasize PDX1+NKX6.1+ as a stronger checkpoint than PDX1 alone.
- Endocrine induction studies converge on Notch inhibition and TGF-beta pathway modulation.
- Maturation studies emphasize 3D clustering, T3, extended culture, and functional GSIS rather than insulin staining alone.

Self-check and limitations:
- Exact factor names differ across protocols because different studies use different inhibitors for the same pathway.
- Some papers use proprietary media or kit-like formulations, which limits exact reconstruction of every component.
- Marker timing varies across iPSC lines, so the DOE includes harvest-day windows rather than one rigid day for every stage.
- I treated beta-cell differentiation as a staged continuum and prioritized checkpoints that are most predictive of final function.
- The most likely first source of variability will be CHIR/Wnt response during definitive endoderm and RA/TPB response during pancreatic progenitor specification.

Bottom-line recommendation:
- Start with a six-stage directed differentiation backbone.
- Optimize definitive endoderm and PDX1+NKX6.1+ pancreatic progenitor formation first.
- Then optimize endocrine induction.
- Finally optimize 3D maturation and GSIS.
